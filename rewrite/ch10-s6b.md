This Shylock-like clinging[^118] to the letter of the law of 1844, insofar as it regulated children’s labor, was only the lead-up to an open revolt against the same law, insofar as it regulated the labor of “young persons and women.” The reader will remember that the chief aim and object of that law was to abolish the “false relay system.” The employers began their revolt with a simple declaration. The sections of the Act of 1844 that prohibited using young persons and women at will, in whatever short fractions of the 15-hour day the employer chose, had been “comparatively harmless,” they said, so long as the work-time was fixed at 12 hours. But under the Ten Hours’ Act they were a “grievous hardship.”[^119] In the coolest manner, they informed the inspectors that they would place themselves above the letter of the law and bring back the old system on their own account.[^120] They were doing this, they said, in the interests of the ill-advised factory workers themselves, “in order to be able to pay them higher wages.”

But quite apart from actual overwork, this so-called relay system was a product of capitalist fantasy that Fourier, in his humorous sketches of “Courses Seances” [short sessions], never outdid, except that the “attraction of labour” was turned into the attraction of capital. Look, for example, at those schemes of the employers that the “respectable” press praised as models of “what a reasonable degree of care and method can accomplish.” The *personnel* of the workers was sometimes divided into anywhere from 12 to 14 categories, and these categories themselves kept changing and rearranging their members. During the 15 hours of the factory day, capital dragged the worker in now for 30 minutes, now for an hour, and then pushed him out again, only to drag him into the factory and thrust him out once more. It hounded him this way and that, in scattered shreds of time, without ever losing its grip on him until the full 10 hours’ work was done. As on the stage, the same persons had to appear in turn in the different scenes of the different acts. But just as an actor belongs to the stage during the whole course of the play, so the factory workers belonged to the factory during 15 hours, not counting the time for going and coming. So the hours of rest were turned into hours of forced idleness, which drove the young men to the tavern and the girls to the brothel. Day after day the capitalist hit upon new tricks for keeping his machinery going 12 or 15 hours without increasing the number of his workers, and at every new trick the worker had to gulp down his meals now in this fragment of time, now in that. At the time of the 10 hours’ agitation, the employers had cried out that the working mob was petitioning in the hope of getting 12 hours’ wages for 10 hours’ work. Now they turned the medal around. These were the same oily Free-traders, sweating with the love of humanity, who for a full 10 years, during the Anti-Corn Law agitation, had preached to the factory workers, with calculations in pounds, shillings, and pence, that with free importation of grain, and with the means English industry possessed, 10 hours’ labor would be quite enough to make the capitalists rich.[^130] After two years, this revolt of capital was at last crowned with victory by a decision of one of the four highest Courts of Justice in England, the Court of Exchequer. The Act of 1850 changed the 15 hours’ time from 6 a.m. to 8.30 p.m. into the 12 hours from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m., but for “young persons and women” only. So it did not affect children, who could always be employed for half an hour before this period and 2½ hours after it, provided their labor as a whole did not exceed 6½ hours. While the bill was under discussion, the Factory Inspectors laid before Parliament statistics of the infamous abuses caused by this anomaly. It was no use. In the background lurked the intention of using the children to screw up the working day of adult males to 15 hours in prosperous years. The experience of the three following years showed that such an attempt was bound to come to grief against the resistance of the adult male factory workers.
