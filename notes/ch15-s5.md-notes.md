2. ch15-p110 – German machine names Bandmühle, Schnurmühle, Mühlenstuhl (not italic in the source). – Kept as printed, with safest literal glosses [ribbon mill], [cord mill], [mill loom]. – §2.3 (German technical words get a bracketed gloss); §0.11 safest literal gloss, no etymological guessing.

3. ch15-p113 – Latin "quan plures" is presumably a misprint for "quam plures". – Kept as printed. – §2.3: foreign text stays exactly as printed. The existing bracketed English translation was rewritten in plain English; its sense ("than several people") is unaffected.

4. [^115] – "widerspread" is a one-reading typo. – Corrected to "widespread". – §1.4 / §0.7.

5. [^115] – "Bauernlegen" (Engels' German term). – Kept, with no extra bracketed gloss, because Engels himself glosses it immediately before ("the clearing of the estates"). – §2.3 aims at a translation; it is already given in the text, so a second gloss would be padding.

6. [^120] – "Rep. lnsp. Fact." (lowercase L for capital I). – Corrected to "Rep. Insp. Fact." – One-reading character typo in a citation (§1.4, like "p. Ixxi" → "p. lxxi"); [^119] and [^125] print the same title correctly.

7. [^121] – "n. 17, 862 and 17, 863" has stray spaces inside the question numbers (17,862 and 17,863). – Kept as printed. – §1.4 lists "a number with a stray space" as a case that stays as printed and is logged.

8. [^125] – "pp. 108,109" (no space after the comma). – Kept as printed. – Citation copied exactly (§2.6); the number checker tokenizes the printed form.

9. [^117] – "the Poor Law of 1833" (the Poor Law Amendment Act is usually dated 1834). – Kept as printed. – §1.4: never correct facts.

10. ch15-p116, ch15-p117, ch15-p120, [^113] – "manufactures" (plural: manufacturing industries/establishments of the manufacture period). – Kept "manufactures" (glossary: manufacture → manufacture), not "manufacturing workshops" (the glossary rendering for "manufactory" only). – The word is Marx's technical term for the form; it was explained earlier in the chapter.

11. ch15-p116 – "the relatively small number of town operatives handed down from the middle ages". – Rendered "town workers", not the glossary "factory workers". – These are medieval town craftsmen, not factory hands; "factory workers" would be false. The script warns about it; the warning is expected.

12. ch15-p116 – "a means of virtually supplying a deficiency of labourers, and not as a means of actually displacing those in work". – Rendered "making up, in effect, for a shortage of workers … not … actually displacing". – "virtually" here contrasts with "actually" (as in Steuart's "(virtuellement)" in [^114]); "in effect" keeps that contrast. The French "(virtuellement)" in [^114] is translated literally as "(virtually)" (§2.3).

13. ch15-p116 – The long sentence "Long before the period of modern industry, cooperation … gave rise, in numerous countries where these methods were applied in agriculture, to … revolutions…" – Split off the place clause as "This happened in numerous countries where these methods were applied in agriculture." – Meaning risk checked: the clause still restricts the claim to those countries; nothing generalized.

14. ch15-p119 – "Hence, the character of independence and estrangement which the capitalist mode of production as a whole gives … is developed by means of machinery into a thorough antagonism." – Split into "So the capitalist mode of production as a whole gives … ; by means of machinery, this character is developed into a thorough antagonism." – Kept the "Hence" inference ("So") and the "as a whole" scope; the pun "temporal" / "a temporary inconvenience" kept with its quotation marks.

15. ch15-p117 – "a temporary inconvenience" is already in quotation marks in the original; kept. In ch15-p128 the scare-quoted "temporary" is kept. In [^117] the phrase is inside the Prize Essay quotation and stays as printed.

16. ch15-p133 – Marx's bracketed interpolation inside Ure's quotation ("[namely the, in their eyes, burdensome terms of their contracts with the workmen]"). – Rewritten in Marx's plain voice: "[namely the terms of their contracts with the workers, which in their eyes were burdensome]". – §0.9: Marx's interjections inside quotations take the plain style; Ure's words around it stay verbatim.

17. ch15-p137 / ch15-p138 – "refractory hand of labour". – Glossed once inside Ure's quotation as "refractory [unruly]"; in Marx's repetition in p138 kept "the refractory hand of labor" with the original word. – Marx is quoting Ure's "doctrine" back at him (mention, §1.2), so the word must survive.

18. ch15-p135 – "surrender at discretion" glossed "[unconditionally]"; [^114] Ravenstone "abridge" glossed "[shorten]"; [^117] "parochial" glossed "[parish]" and "mechanic" glossed "[skilled craftsman]"; ch15-p121 "heretofore" glossed "[until now]". – §2.1 / §2.2 gloss-don't-replace. "short-timers" (p125) left unglossed: a gloss would require guessing whether children on half-time are meant.

19. ch15-p121 – The original has no space between some footnote markers and the next opening quotation mark ("[^119]“The adaptation…"). – Added a space. – §2.1 allows modernizing punctuation; the markers stay in place.

20. ch15-p122 – "the Zenith year of the English cotton industry". – Rendered "the peak year". – Plain word, same sense; not a quotation.

21. ch15-p144 – "thank Providence, who by means of machinery…". – Kept "who" (personified Providence), and the irony of "dispenser of consolation", "his liberal soul" and the quoted "immortal interests".
