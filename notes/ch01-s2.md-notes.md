2. ch01-p022 – First appearance of "useful labor" (§7). – No §7 explanation added. – Marx defines the term in this very sentence ("we call useful labour"); §7.0 says his own words are the definition.
3. ch01-p035 / ch01-p036 – First appearance of "concrete" labor ("labour of some useful concrete form", "concrete useful labour"). – No §7 explanation added. – Marx is developing the concept right here, and p036 states it outright ("in a special form and with a definite aim ... it produces use values"); a gloss would duplicate his own definition. 4. ch01-p024 – Context file lists "division of labor (in manufacture)" as first appearing here. – No §7 explanation added. – Every use in this file is the *social* division of labor (between branches of production), not division of labor in manufacture; the §7 wording ("splitting the making of one product into many separate tasks") would contradict the sense. The factory example in p024 describes workshop division but does not use the term in the §7 sense, and adding the gloss there would resolve how Marx relates the two. §7.0: the context file matches words, not senses.
5. ch01-p030 [^15] – First appearance of "wages" (§7). Marx says wages is a category that does not yet exist at this stage of the analysis. – Added the §7 wording in parentheses at the second mention ("Wages (the price paid for labor-power, which appears, wrongly, as the price of labor itself) is a category ..."), not at the first ("the wages, or value, that the worker gets"). – The second mention is where Marx treats wages as a category; attaching the gloss to "the wages, or value" would have made the sentence unreadable. The gloss does not contradict Marx's statement that the category is deferred. Kept "Wages ... is a category" (singular) because the word names a category.
