# Notes for ch14-s3.md

1. ch14-p015 – "modern industry" first technical use. – §7 explanation added as a separate bracketed sentence after "modern industry carried on by machinery". – Keeps Marx's own phrase intact; the explanation is the §7 wording verbatim.

2. ch14-p016, p018, n. 19, n. 23 – "modern mechanical industry" / Ure's capitalized "Modern Mechanical Industry" are not glossary variants. – Kept as printed (capitals kept in n. 23, where the phrase echoes Ure's own title-like usage). – Glossary covers only "modern industry"; changing these would alter wording without need.

3. ch14-p016 – French watch-trade names. – Kept exactly, each with a literal bracketed gloss; Marx's own parenthetical descriptions kept as well. Unsure glosses (safest literal, §0.11): planteur de finissage [finishing setter], repasseur de barillet [barrel re-finisher], repasseur [re-finisher] ("repasser" = go over again), faiseur de secret [secret maker], établisseur [establisher], ciseleur [chaser]. "raquette makers" left without a bracket: Marx treats "raquette" as an English trade word and already explains it. "membra disjecta" glossed [scattered limbs].

4. ch14-p016 – Long "because ... and because ..., and the capitalist, by scattering the work, saves ..." sentence. – Kept the third clause attached to the second "because" clause, without adding a third "because". – Adding one would settle whether the savings on workshops is a separate cause; the original leaves the grammar loose.

5. [^7] – "modem industry" → "modern industry". One-reading typo (§1.4), fixed. "La Chaux-de-Fond" (usually "La Chaux-de-Fonds") kept as printed: place name, not clearly a typo of this edition.

6. [^19] – "learned men, handicraftsman, and even peasants" → "craftsmen" (plural). One-reading typo (list of classes), fixed.

8. ch14-p020 – "price" listed as first appearance, but the only use is "market-price", introduced by Marx with "to express ourselves superficially". – No §7 explanation added. – The §7 definition (money name of value) would contradict or over-specify a deliberately superficial market-price remark (§7.0 "never contradict the text"). Price should be explained at its first technical use in a later file/chapter.

9. ch14-p020 – "socially necessary" (without "labour-time") is the first technical use of socially necessary labor-time. – Rendered "the socially necessary labor-time for its production" with the §7 explanation in parentheses, keeping the "since"-clause about competition as "For, to put it superficially, ...". – The term is the same concept; naming it fully lets the explanation attach.

10. ch14-p025 vs p028 – "necessary labour-time" in p025 means socially necessary time for producing commodities, not the necessary part of the working day. – §7 "necessary labor" explanation placed at p028 (labor-time required for the reproduction of labor-power), its first technical use; p025 left unexplained and worded "the necessary labor-time" as printed. – §7.0 first technical use.

11. ch14-p028 – Six first-use explanations fall in this paragraph (value, wages, value of labor-power, surplus-value, necessary labor, surplus labor). – Each added as a separate bracketed sentence right after the sentence containing the term, so none alters Marx's own sentences; necessary/surplus labor combined in one bracket after the final sentence. "value" explained at "different values" (of labor-powers), its first technical use in the chapter (Ure's "appropriate value" in n. 23 is inside a quotation and later in reading order).

12. ch14-p028 – "the individual labourers are appropriated and annexed for life". – Kept "appropriated" rather than a plainer verb. – It echoes Ure's "This appropriation ..." quoted in n. 23 attached to this sentence; the echo would be lost.

13. ch14-p028 – "more pregnant moments of activity" → "weightier elements of activity" (§3.2 "moment" → element); "ossified" → "hardened"; "the latter/the former" → "the unskilled/the skilled" (§3.3).
