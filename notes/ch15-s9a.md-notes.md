# Notes for ch15-s9a.md

1. ch15-p247 – "Now, if the Factory Acts … indirectly hasten on … so, if it were made obligatory …" is a two-part conditional. Splitting it into a plain statement plus a new "if" sentence would turn Marx's first "if" into a flat assertion. – Kept one sentence: "Now, if the Factory Acts … and so indirectly attack … then in the same way, if it were made compulsory …". "at one full swoop" (a variant of "fell swoop") → "at a single stroke". – §1.1 keep strength; ambiguity beats sentence length (§1.2).

2. ch15-p252 – "one grown up, renters" – "renters" makes no sense here; the 1887 text reads "tenters" (machine minders), and the quoted report in p253 speaks of the "machine minder". – Corrected to "tenters" and added "(machine minders)" as a neutral identification. – One-reading typo (§1.4); the word is undecodable for a modern reader without the gloss (§1.1 c). Meaning risk: low.

3. ch15-p261 – "equal restrain on all exploitation" (Marx's voice). – Corrected to "equal restraint". – One-reading typo (§1.4).

4. ch15-p261 – "in a, comparatively speaking, wonderfully short time": stray comma after "a". – Dropped it ("in what is, comparatively speaking, a wonderfully short time"). – One-reading stray punctuation (§1.4).

5. ch15-p255 – Sentence begins with lowercase "modern industry rent the veil". – Capitalized ("Modern industry tore away the veil"). – One-reading typo (§1.4).

6. ch15-p251 [^221] – "the amount of tambour-power expended": "tambour-power" is not a term; the sense (and the German "Arbeitskraft") is labour-power. – Corrected to "labor-power". 8. ch15-p262 – "an unjustice and loss to us" inside the Cooksley quotation. – Kept as printed. – It is inside a quotation (§2.1 near-verbatim) and may be the witnesses' or report's own wording; not fixed in quoted evidence.

10. ch15-p258 / ch15-p259 / ch15-p267 – "home-labour" / "home-work": the glossary maps "home-work" to "domestic industry", but Marx marks these as someone else's term ("the so-called 'home-labour'", "the so-called home-work"). – Kept "home-labour" in quotation marks in p258 (as printed), "home-labor" (American spelling, unquoted, as printed) in p259 where it stands in Marx's voice, and "so-called home-work" in p267. "domestic industries" in n. 241 stays. – Mention vs use (§1.2): "so-called" flags the word itself.

11. ch15-p258 – "patria potestas" (Latin, not italic in the source). – Kept as printed, without a bracketed translation. – Marx glosses it himself immediately ("on parental authority"); a second gloss would be redundant.

12. ch15-p257 – "Ne sutor ultra crepidam – this nec plus ultra of handicraft wisdom". §3.4 says nec plus ultra → "the very height", but Marx is punning on "ultra" from the proverb. – Kept "*nec plus ultra*" with a bracket [very height]; translated the proverb literally [Let the shoemaker not go beyond his shoe]; "écoles d'enseignement professionnel" → [schools of vocational education]. – Replacing the tag would lose the wordplay (mention vs use, §1.2; famous-tag rule §2.3). Italics added to nec plus ultra as a foreign tag.

13. ch15-p269 – *experimentum in corpore vili*. – Kept, with literal bracket [experiment on a worthless body]. – Famous tag (§2.3).

14. ch15-p258 / p263 / p265 – "Ch. Empl. Comm." in Marx's running prose. – Expanded to "Children's Employment Commission"; citations in notes keep the abbreviation exactly. – Readability; citation rule §2.6 applies only to citations.

15. ch15-p256 – "the detail-worker of to-day, grappled by life-long repetition". – Rendered "held in the grip of lifelong repetition", not "crippled". – "grappled" means seized/held; "crippled" would add a claim. The split of this long final sentence into three keeps each element (fragment of a man; fit for a variety of labors; functions as modes of free play) attached to the right subject.

16. ch15-p256 – "But if modern industry, by its very nature, therefore necessitates … on the other hand, in its capitalistic form, it reproduces …" and "But if, on the one hand … modern industry, on the other hand …". – Rendered as "So modern industry … requires … But on the other hand …" and "On the one hand … But on the other hand …". – The concessive "if" here is not hypothetical; the "therefore" is kept as "So". Meaning risk: low.
