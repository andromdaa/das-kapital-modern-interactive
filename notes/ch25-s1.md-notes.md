# Notes: ch25-s1.md

Format: paragraph ID – the problem – what I decided – why.

1. ch25-p002/p003 – "composition of capital" (and constant/variable capital) is listed as a first appearance, but Marx defines value-composition, technical composition, and organic composition himself right here, and glosses constant capital as "value of the means of production" and variable capital as "value of labour power, the sum total of wages". – No §7 explanation for composition of capital, constant capital, or variable capital; kept Marx's own definitions. Kept "*value-composition*" (p002, hyphenated, italic) and "value composition" (p003) as printed, since he is coining the terms. – §7.0: "Never contradict the text"; his words are the definition.
2. ch25-p002 – Value, means of production, labor-power, value of labor-power, means of subsistence, and wages all first appear in one sentence. – Kept Marx's sentence intact and added the §7 wordings as short separate sentences right after it ("Here value means…", "The means of production are…", etc.), with means of subsistence explained inside the value-of-labor-power sentence. – Putting six explanations inside Marx's sentence would bury his two-part definition; separate sentences keep his argument readable and clearly distinct from the additions. Meaning risk: none of the additions touches the value/material distinction he draws.
3. ch25-p001 – "accumulation" explanation mentions surplus-value, which is only explained at its first use in p005. – Accepted the forward reference. – §7 wording is verbatim; surplus-value gets its own explanation at its first textual use.
4. ch25-p002 – "living labour power" is listed under "dead labor / living labor". – No explanation; rendered "living labor-power". – Not the dead/living labor contrast; it just means the labor-power actually at work (§7.0, word match only).
5. [^3] – "long working days" in Marx's bracketed interjection; "working day" is listed as a first appearance. – No §7 explanation. – Everyday use inside a short aside; the phrase is clear and the explanation would bring in necessary/surplus labor in the middle of a quoted citation. The full surplus-labor explanation in p015 mentions the working day.
6. ch25-p013 – "means of payment" listed as a first appearance. – No §7 explanation; term kept. – Here it means wage money flowing back to workers. The §7 wording (settling a debt after goods changed hands on credit) would confuse the reader and arguably impose a reading Marx does not develop here. Logged as a meaning risk.
7. ch25-p014 – "price" first technical use. "price of labour" appears earlier (p013) but is the irrational expression Marx keeps exactly, not commodity price. – Explained price at "price of commodities" in p014, as a short parenthetical sentence after the crisis/prosperity sentence. – §7.0 first technical use; a parenthesis inside the sentence would split the crisis/prosperity parallel.
8. ch25-p014 – "circulation" explained at "money … is in circulation". – Added the §7 wording in parentheses after "circulation". Kept the [^8] marker right after "too little", where it stands in the original. – The currency school's claim concerns circulation in Marx's technical sense.
9. ch25-p015 – "metamorphosed by economists into pretended law of Nature" matched "metamorphosis". – Kept "metamorphosed", no explanation. – Everyday sense (turned into), not the C–M / M–C change of form (§7.0).
10. ch25-p015 – Surplus-labor explanation uses "necessary labor" and "working day", neither explained in this chapter. – Kept the §7 wording verbatim. – Cutting it would leave the explanation incomplete; both terms are readable in context.
11. ch25-p006 – "Classical economy" matched "classical political economy". – Rendered "Classical economics" (§0.10) with the §7 explanation, dropping "in Marx's view" since this is Marx's own voice. – Same practice as earlier rewritten chapters.
12. "truck system" – listed as a first appearance, but the only match is "struck" in [^6] ("struck the hour of the Protestant parsons"). – Nothing done. – False word match.
13. [^1] – "Karl Marx, l. c." stands before a quotation that is actually from Colins (as the following citation says). – Kept as printed ("Karl Marx, cited above."). – Unclear source oddity, not a one-reading typo (§1.4).
14. [^1] – French quotation from Colins. – Translated literally: "[With the oppression of the masses being equal, the more proletarians a country has, the richer it is.]" Kept Marx's "prolétarian" (accent, mention of the word) as printed. – §2.3; §1.2 mention vs use.
15. [^1] – "orang-outang", "scrofulous" in Marx's voice. – "orangutan" (modern spelling); kept "scrofulous" with "[tubercular]". – Spelling modernization only; "scrofulous" is half of a sneer paired with "syphilitic", so the word stays and gets a gloss.

19. [^6] – Petty's "mortified" and "Benefices". – Glossed once: "mortified [held down by self-denial]", "Benefices [paid church posts]". Kept Marx's scare-quoted "mortify" unglossed. – Marx's pun depends on the word; the gloss gives the old sense without replacing it (§2.2).
20. [^6] – "(sc. Hume)" and "(viz., by his “Theory of Moral Sentiments”)" inside the bishop's quoted words. – Both rendered "namely". – Treated as Marx's interjections (§0.9). "sc." (scilicet) is not on the §3.4 list; "namely" is its literal sense. The quoted words around them are unchanged.
21. [^6] – Long quotations (Petty, Bishop Horne, Smith, Anderson) and the open quotation after "Adam Smith, “had the atrocious wickedness…" with a nested “Theory of Moral Sentiments”. – Copied verbatim, including the unmatched quotation marks. – §2.2, §1.4.
22. ch25-p013 – "the master is always master" (Marx paraphrasing Adam Smith). – Kept "master", not "employer". – The phrase is a play on the word ("master is always master"); "employer" would lose it (§1.2 mention).
23. ch25-p013 – "peculium" in Marx's voice. – Kept with a bracket "[a slave's private savings]". – Roman-law term; Marx's comparison with the slave depends on it. Neutral identification.
24. ch25-p005 – Long "Since…; since…; since lastly…, the requirements … may exceed" sentence. – Split into three statements followed by "Because of all this, the requirements … may exceed…". – Keeps Marx's causal link to all three premises; "may" kept in each place.
25. ch25-p014 – "the diminished rate either of the absolute, or of the proportional, increase in labour power, or labouring population" (and the matching "increased rate" clause). – "a reduced / an increased rate of increase in labor-power, or in the working population, whether absolute or proportional". – Keeps both alternatives open without choosing between absolute and proportional.
26. Section 1 heading – American spelling in the heading. – Only "labour" → "labor"; kept "labor power" without hyphen and the odd capitalization as printed. – §1.4 headings rule.
