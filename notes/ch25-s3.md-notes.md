Format: paragraph ID – the problem – what I decided – why.

1. ch25-p034 / ch25-p035 – "relative surplus population" is listed as a first appearance. Marx defines the concept himself at the end of p034 ("a relatively redundant population of labourers, i.e., a population of greater extent than suffices for the average needs of the self-expansion of capital, and therefore a surplus population") and names it in p035 as the result of that definition. – No §7 explanation added. – §7.0 "Never contradict the text": Marx is defining and developing the term in this very passage; the §7 wording would duplicate and partly pre-empt his definition (e.g. "press down on the employed", which he argues only in p047/p051).
2. ch25-p036 – "industrial reserve army" (first appearance). Marx defines it in the same sentences ("a disposable industrial reserve army, that belongs to capital ... a mass of human material always ready for exploitation"). – No §7 explanation added. – Same reason as note 1; the section heading is the concept and the whole section develops it.

[^19] – "bourgeois" first appears in Marx's voice in the footnote ("his usual unerring bourgeois instinct"). – Added the §7 wording in a parenthesis after the phrase. – §7.0: first technical use in the chapter (class sense), in Marx's own voice, not inside a quotation. The mocking tone of "unerring" is kept.
4. [^17] – "l. c., p, 107, et seq." – Rendered "cited above, p. 107, and following"; fixed "p," to "p.". – One-reading punctuation typo (§1.4); "et seq." expanded like "seq." (§2.6).
5. [^14] – "Coal-mining: 1851, 183,389, 1861, 246,613, “The increase…" uses commas where the rest of the list uses semicolons/periods. – Left as printed. – More than one possible correction (semicolon or period); §1.4 says unclear oddities stay.
6. [^14] – "Chandlery" (census category name). – Kept the word and added "(candle-making)". – A modern reader cannot decode it; the footnote is Marx's prose listing census headings, so I kept the heading and added a neutral identification rather than replacing it.
7. [^16] – "Vol. I , p. 146" has a stray space before the comma. – Left as printed. – Inside a citation, which is copied exactly (§2.6); harmless.
8. ch25-p036 – "decennial cycle" (also p048 "decennial cycles"). – Rendered "ten-year cycle(s)". – Plain equivalent, same meaning.
9. ch25-p036 – "unemployed or half-employed hands" in Marx's voice. – Rendered "workers". – Glossary: "hands" only in quotations or where Marx mocks the employers' word; no scare quotes or mockery here.
10. ch25-p036 – "The composition of capital changed but very slowly." follows the sentence on "the childhood of capitalist production". – Kept as a separate sentence without adding "then" or "at that time"; the "So" before the next sentence reproduces Marx's "therefore". – No link added beyond the original.
11. ch25-p040 – "beau ideal" in quotation marks, Marx's ironic tag. – Kept it in quotation marks with "[perfect model]". – Foreign phrase used as a quoted tag; §2.3 bracketed translation. Political economy keeps its personification as "she" and "an old maid".
12. ch25-p044 – "In the latter case ... in the former ..." – Renamed the cases: "With the greater number ... with the smaller number". – §3.3 "the former / the latter": name the thing. "Latter" = greater number of labourers, "former" = smaller number; checked against the sentence before.
13. ch25-p047 – "proportioned to the different sections of the working class according to age and sex". – Rendered "adjusted to the different sections ... according to age and sex", not "shared out among". – "Shared out" would have changed the meaning (distribution of a total) instead of adjusting the amount of labor per section. Meaning risk handled by staying close to the original verb.
14. ch25-p048 – The long sentence "For Modern Industry ... that would indeed be a beautiful law, which pretends ... instead of regulating ..." – Split off the description of modern industry into its own sentence, but kept "which pretends ... instead of regulating ..." in one sentence. – "Instead of regulating" has an unclear subject (the law? capital's action?); splitting further would force one reading (§0.6). Irony ("beautiful law") kept.
15. ch25-p048 – The economists' dogma runs over several sentences after "According to them". – Did not add repeated "they say" tags; the sequence is unbroken in the original and "Or, as others explain it" keeps the attribution visible, and Marx's own verdict ("A beautiful mode of motion, this…!") stays clearly separate as an exclamation.
16. ch25-p049 – "a rise of wages practically insignificant, though accompanied by falling prices of corn". – Kept the "though" clause in the same position: "a rise of wages that was practically insignificant, though accompanied by falling prices of grain". – Marx does not say whether "though" qualifies "insignificant" or the rise as such; the order keeps both readings. "corn" → "grain" (§3.3).
17. ch25-p049 – "the demands of war" (the Crimean War). – Not identified. – Marx does not name the war; the passage can be followed without it (§1.1 c).
18. ch25-p050 – "according to its varying needs" – Kept "its" ("according to its changing needs"). – Could refer to capital or to the working population's distribution; keeping the pronoun leaves Marx's reference as open as the original.
19. ch25-p051 – "it holds its pretensions in check" – Rendered "it holds the active army's claims in check". – In context "its" can only be the active labor-army (the reserve army is the subject); one-reading pronoun, named for clarity. "paroxysm" → "frenzy".
20. ch25-p052 – "economic apologetics". – Added "the defense of the system" once, following the §3.3 rendering for "the apologists (defenders of the system)". – High-school reader may not know the word; neutral, no evaluation added.
21. ch25-p052 – "Les dés sont pipés." (French, Marx's voice). – Kept as printed with "[The dice are loaded]". – §2.3; literal translation.
22. ch25-p053 – "As soon ... as soon as ... as soon as ..., so soon capital ... cry out". – Kept it as one long sentence with semicolons and "just as soon". – Splitting into separate sentences would break the "as soon as ... so soon" logic that ties the outcry to the workers' actions.
23. ch25-p053 – "its commonplace Sancho Panza". – Kept the image and added "(the down-to-earth squire of Don Quixote)" once. – §1.1 c / §1.3: the image depends on a literary reference a high-school reader may not have; the gloss is neutral and does not explain the joke (that political economy is capital's sidekick).
24. ch25-p053 – "Trades' Unions" – Kept (keep-list §4 "trades' unions"), with Marx's capitalization.
25. [^15] – Engels' addition: "is conjectured rather than understood by some of the prominent economists". – Rendered "guessed at, rather than understood". "Lond. 1833" outside the title → "London 1833"; "l. c." → "cited above"; "Ibid." → "Same source". Barton, Ricardo, Jones, Ramsay quotations kept verbatim, including "arts" (readable as crafts/industrial skills in context, not glossed).
26. [^19] – "consequence" inside the "Essay on Trade and Commerce" quotation means importance. – Glossed "consequence [importance]". – §2.1/2.2: a modern reader would read it as "result".
27. [^19] – "the operative cotton-spinners of Blackburn" in Marx's voice. – Rendered "the cotton-spinning factory workers of Blackburn" (glossary: operative → factory worker). Inside the pamphlet quotation "operatives", "hands" and "masters" are kept. Marx's tag "it goes on to say" rendered "the pamphlet goes on to say", which names the existing subject without changing the speaker.
