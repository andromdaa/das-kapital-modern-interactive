1. Heading "Chapter 16: Absolute and Relative Surplus- Value" – stray space inside "Surplus- Value". – Kept as printed. – §1.4 says heading wording stays as printed (only American spelling applied); the more specific heading rule wins over the general typo permission.

2. ch16-p001 – "means of production" first appears inside Marx's quotation of his own Ch. 7. – Kept the quotation verbatim (with "subject of labour [the material worked on]" glossed, per the §3.1 note) and put the §7 explanation in a parenthesis after the quoted sentence, in Marx's voice. – §1.1 (c) and §0.8 forbid additions inside quotations.

3. ch16-p002 – "collective labourer" is listed as a first appearance. – No §7 explanation added. – Marx defines it right there ("that is, by a combination of workers, each of whom takes only a part…"); §7.0 says his own words are the definition.

4. ch16-p004 – "absolute surplus-value", "relative surplus-value", "necessary labor", "surplus labor" are listed as first appearances. – No §7 explanations added for them. – Marx defines all four in this very paragraph (lengthening the day beyond the equivalent of the value of labor-power = absolute; shortening necessary labor to lengthen surplus labor = relative; the day divided into necessary and surplus labor). Explanations were added for working day, labor-power, value of labor-power, value, and (later in the paragraph) wages.

5. ch16-p004 – "means of subsistence" is used inside the §7 explanation of value of labor-power before its first appearance in Marx's text (p008). – Gave the means-of-subsistence explanation at its first use in Marx's own text (p008). – §7.0 counts Marx's uses, not the wording of added explanations.

6. ch16-p004 – "the composition of society" rendered "the makeup of society". – Avoided "composition", which in this book is a technical term (composition of capital) and would mislead. Meaning unchanged.

7. ch16-p006 – "so far as it is propagandist". – Kept the word "propagandist" without an added explanation. – Any paraphrase ("winning new ground", "spreading itself") would interpret the image; the word is understandable as it stands.

8. ch16-p006 – Marx's "If, on the one hand, … so, on the other hand, …" is a parallel, not a hypothetical. – Split into "On the one hand … For example … On the other hand …". – Meaning risk checked: the original "if" states an accepted fact (formal subjection suffices), not an assumption, so no hypothetical was lost.

9. ch16-p008 – "for himself and his race". – Rendered "for himself and his kind". – "race" here is the older sense (his offspring / his line or kind, German *Race*), not ethnic race; a modern reader would misread it. "kind" keeps the open scope (descendants or fellows) instead of narrowing it to "children" or "family". In ch16-p010 "(race, &c.)" refers to man's physical constitution and is kept as "race".

10. ch16-p009 – "any more … than unconquerable natural obstacle prevent one man from eating the flesh of another" – singular "obstacle" with plural verb "prevent". – Rendered "obstacles prevent". – One-reading typo (missing "s"; the earlier "no natural obstacle … preventing" makes the plural the only sense here) (§1.4).

11. ch16-p014 – "leading-strings" inside a quotation. – Kept, with the gloss "[strings used to guide a small child learning to walk]". – §2.1/§2.2: gloss, don't replace.

12. ch16-p014 – "Examples are, the irrigation works in Egypt, Lombardy, Holland, or in India and Persia where irrigation by means of artificial canals, not only supplies …" – Split, starting a new sentence with "There, irrigation …". – Meaning risk: the "where" clause could attach to India and Persia only or to the whole list. "There," directly after "in India and Persia" reads most naturally as referring to those two, as the original does, and does not extend the claim to Egypt, Lombardy, and Holland.

13. ch16-p014 – "social division of labour" is the division of labor in society, not the division of labor *in manufacture* listed in §7. – No §7 explanation added. – §7.0: explain only at the first use in the listed technical sense. Mill's "division of employments" (p026) is inside a quotation.
