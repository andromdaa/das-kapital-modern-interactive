# Notes for ch14-s4.md

1. ch14-p030 – *genera* is Marx's own Latin (biological) word, and the passage goes on with "families", "species", "sub-species". – Kept *genera* with a literal gloss [kinds], and kept "families", "species", "sub-species". – The classification image runs through the sentence. §2.3 gives the safest literal gloss.

2. [^25] – The French Skarbek quotation has "division de la besogne on de travail", where "on" is almost certainly a misprint for "ou". – Left the French as printed and translated "or", as the source's own bracketed translation does. – §2.3: foreign text is kept exactly. Only the translation is plain English.

3. [^25] – The source's bracketed translation says "manufactories and workshops". – Rewrote it as "manufacturing establishments and workshops" (the script warns that the "manufacturing workshop" rendering is missing). – "manufacturing workshops and workshops" reads like a slip. "establishments" keeps both of the French nouns (*manufactures*, *ateliers*) and adds nothing. I also used American spelling in the rewritten bracketed translation ("civilization", "labor"), because it is the editors' or our translation, not the quoted author's words.

4. ch14-p031 – "means of subsistence" is listed as a first appearance in this chapter. – Added the §7 explanation at "different means of subsistence", where the term is used technically next to "means of production". – §7.0.

5. ch14-p031 – "In the latter case … In the former" – Rendered as "In this second case … In the first case". – I checked the reference: "the latter" is exchange between distinct communities, "the former" is the physiological division. "latter/former" become plain ordinals, following §3.3.

6. ch14-p034 – "circulation of commodities" is listed as a first appearance. – Added the §7 explanation in parentheses after "the production and the circulation of commodities". – Technical use (the precondition of the capitalist mode of production).

7. ch14-p034 – "split up into numerous, and to some extent, entirely new manufactures" – Rendered "numerous, and to some extent entirely new, branches of manufacture". – A first draft had "some of them entirely new", but that would settle the ambiguity of "to some extent" (only some of the branches, or partly new). I reverted to Marx's hedge (§1.1).

8. ch14-p034 – Ferguson's "Helots" (inside a quotation). – Kept the word and added the gloss [the serfs of ancient Sparta]. – §2.1/§2.2: gloss, don't replace, inside quotations. The comparison makes no sense without it.

9. [^31] – The Berkeley quotation has a closing quotation mark and no opening one. "Whitney" is probably Witney, and "soies" is an unclear cloth name. – Left all three as printed. Added no gloss for "soies", "long ells", "crapes", or "linseys". – §1.4: an unmatched quotation mark has more than one reading. These are trade words inside a quotation, and I won't guess at "soies" (§0.11).

10. ch14-p035 – "use-value", "bourgeois", and "factory system" are listed as first appearances. – Added the §7 wording at each: use-value in parentheses after "produce a use-value"; bourgeois in parentheses after "The same bourgeois mind"; factory system between dashes after "the enthusiastic apologists of the factory system". – §7.0. All three are technical uses. The factory-system explanation sits mid-sentence so that it does not come after the ironic punchline ("one immense factory") and blunt it.

11. ch14-p035 – Adam Smith's view (the difference is "merely subjective") is reported, and the original sentence runs on to give the observer's reasons. – Split the sentence and started the second half with "On this view". – Repeats the attribution so that the "merely subjective" claim does not read as Marx's own (§1.2).

12. ch14-p035 – "*a priori* system" / "*a posteriori*, nature-imposed necessity" – Rendered "according to a plan fixed in advance" / "a necessity imposed by nature after the fact" (§3.2). Split the sentence in two, keeping "Within the workshop … Within society" so the contrast stays. – Glossary rendering.

13. ch14-p035 – *bellum omnium contra omnes* [war of all against all – Hobbes] – Kept the Latin and the source's bracket unchanged. – The bracket is already plain English (§2.3).

14. ch14-p036 – The long "If … we find, on the contrary, …" sentence. – Kept the "If" clause attached to its main clause ("we find the opposite in …"), and only then split off "There we find, on the one hand … on the other …". – Splitting off the "If" clause as a flat statement would make the conditional unconditional.

15. ch14-p037 – "Asiatic societies", "Asiatic States" – Kept "Asiatic". – It is a geographical and period term, not an ethnic or religious name (§4), and it is Marx's analytical usage. "Brahmin" is kept as the name of an office.

16. ch14-p037 – The long sentence "The simplicity of the organisation … [^38] ‒ this simplicity supplies the key …". – Kept the dash structure ("Take the simplicity … [^38] – this simplicity supplies the key …"). The footnote stays after "with the same name", as in the original. – Splitting it into separate sentences put the footnote marker and the dash in an awkward position. "Take" is only an opening for the noun phrase and adds no claim.

17. ch14-p037 – "common tanks" (in India, reservoirs) – Kept "tanks". – A modern reader can still follow "distributes water for irrigation from the common tanks", so no identification is needed (§1.1 c: only when needed).

18. [^35] – *mixtum compositum* in Marx's own voice (footnote prose). – Rendered "hodgepodge". – §2.3: a Latin set phrase in Marx's voice that is not a famous tag. It is not in the §3.4 list, so it is logged here. "Yankees" is kept: it is Marx's colloquial and ironic word for Americans, not an ethnic slur covered by §4.

19. [^35] – "with all their belongings" – Rendered "with all their accessories". – "belongings" here means the umbrella's fittings, not personal property. "civil war" is kept lowercase as printed.

20. [^36] – "“Misère,” &c." – "&c." is outside the quoted short title, so it is expanded to "etc." (§2.6). The title itself is unchanged.
