# Notes for ch10-s4.md

1. ch10-p085 – First appearance of "constant capital" in Ch. 10. Marx gives only an apposition ("Constant capital, the means of production"), which identifies but does not define the term. – Kept his apposition and added the §7 wording as a short parenthetical sentence right after the first sentence. – §7.0 first-use rule; the explanation does not contradict the apposition.

2. ch10-p085 – First appearance of "relay system" in Ch. 10. – Did NOT add the §7 explanation. – Marx defines the term himself right there ("this relay system, this alternation of two sets of workers"); §7.0 says not to add an explanation where Marx is defining the term. The §7 clause about legal hours being hard to check belongs to a later context and would be an addition here.

3. ch10-p085 – "the English cotton manufacture" (ordinary sense: the cotton industry, not the Part 4 period). – Rendered "the English cotton industry". – §7.0: everyday sense, no explanation; "manufacture" is not script-enforced.

4. ch10-p085 – "only acts as a palliative". – Rendered "is only a partial remedy". – Simplification risk: "palliative" means easing a problem without curing it; "partial remedy" keeps that sense and the next sentence ("only to a slight degree") keeps the strength.

5. ch10-p085 – "inherent tendency" rendered "built-in tendency" (§3.2 pattern for "immanent"); "therefore" kept as "So".

6. ch10-p089 – The quotation opens with a straight double quote and closes with a curly one. – Changed the opening mark to “. – One-reading punctuation fix (§1.4 / §2.1 modernize punctuation).

7. ch10-p089 – "who, when I asked him ..., 'I daresay, sir, you know that as well as I do,' and admitted the fact" lacks a verb ("said"/"replied"). – Kept as printed. – It is inside a commissioner's quotation; supplying a verb is more than a one-reading typo fix.

8. ch10-p090 – Added the §2.1 dialect gloss "gi’en [given] over" (the existing "[near]" after "gain" kept). "&c." after the closing quotation mark is Marx's voice → "etc.".

9. [^64] – "l.c.. Fourth Report (1865). 58. p. xii." – Expanded to "cited above, Fourth Report (1865). 58. p. xii."; the doubled period disappears with the expansion. Rest of the citation as printed.

10. [^67] – "Fourth Report, &c.. 1865, 79, p. xvi." – Kept exactly, including "&c.." – Abbreviated title; §2.6 keeps citations as printed, and the double period could be either "&c., 1865" or "&c. 1865", so it is not a one-reading typo.

11. [^62] – "Light assists in the elaboration of good plastic blood". – Added the gloss "plastic [tissue-forming]". – A modern reader would read "plastic" as the material; §2.1 gloss, not replacement. Also "their retainers" kept as printed (old-fashioned but clear).

12. [^62] – "(l.c., 284., p. 55.)" – Kept "284." with its period. – Same separator style as other citations here ("80. p. xvi."); not clearly a typo.

13. [^66] – Marx's scare-quoted “labour-powers” (his own irony, not a quotation of someone else) → “labor-powers”, American spelling, scare quotes kept. "degree of culture" → "level of culture" (kept "culture", not "education", to avoid narrowing the sense).

14. ch10-p093, ch10-p098 – Marx's interjections "*(i.e*., of restrictions on night-work)" and "*(*i.e.*,* Ellis Brown & Co. ...)". – "i.e." → "that is" (Marx's voice, §3.4) with the odd italic markup mirrored exactly; "labour-power" → "labor-power" inside Marx's interjection. The "i.e.*,*" inside the firm's own quoted words in p093 kept as printed.

15. ch10-p098 – "Ellis Brown & Co." (the firm is John Brown & Co.; Ellis is the partner). – Kept as printed. – Could be Marx's deliberate fusion or a slip; more than one reading.

16. ch10-p098 – Marx's interjections: "(What mealy-mouthed phraseology!)" → "(What mealy-mouthed wording!)"; "fatal perplexity" → "fatal predicament"; "obliged to pay" → "having to pay". "(What cynical naïveté!)" kept.

17. ch10-p099 – "are concocted on the same large scale". – Rendered "are set up on the same large scale". – Simplification risk: "concocted" may carry a faint sneer, but in context it means "laid out/organized"; the irony of the paragraph is carried by "found it convenient to suppress" and "the Messrs. Cyclops", both kept. "MS." → "manuscript".

18. ch10-p101 – Marx's interjection "(what queer-headed folk!)". – Rendered "(what odd-headed folk!)". – "queer" would be misread today; "odd-headed" keeps the mocking exclamation without strengthening it.

19. ch10-p106 – "a certain number of hands" (Marx's own voice, no quotation marks). – Rendered "workers" per §3.1 ("hands" in quotes only where quoted or mocked). "cheque" → "check drawn on" (American spelling; "drawn on" keeps the banking image clear). The clause order "lose their character as capital, are therefore a pure loss ..., as soon as ..." was reordered into "as soon as ..., they lose ... and so are a pure loss", keeping "therefore" as "and so".

20. ch10-p106 – "In addition to this, it would throw night-labour on those who worked instead of the boys, which they would not be able to stand." – Kept as a plain statement in the same position, as in the original (Marx is relaying Sanderson's argument without a tag); no tag added or removed.
