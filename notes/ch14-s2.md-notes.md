### ch14-s2

- **ch14-p008** – "detail worker" and "collective worker" (§7, first in chapter) – no §7 gloss added – Marx defines both right here: the detail worker as "a labourer who all his life performs one and the same simple operation" (the next "such specialised detail labourers" points back to it), and the collective worker as the one "who constitutes the living mechanism of manufacture". §7.0 says not to add the explanation where Marx gives his own definition.
- **ch14-p008** – "the productive power of labour is increased" – rendered "productive power of labor" (glossary). The §7 gloss for "productivity of labor" goes at p013, where that term ("productiveness of labour") first appears.
- **ch14-p010** – "capital" (§7, first in chapter) – no gloss – its only use in this file is inside the Murray/Wilson quotation ("produced without capital"). §2 forbids additions inside quotations except glosses of undecodable words, and "capital" is readable there in its everyday sense. Later files of Ch. 14 should treat "capital" as not yet explained, even though the context script will list it as already seen.
- **n. 3** – "political economy" (§7, first in chapter) – no gloss – it appears only in the book title "Popular Political Economy". Later Ch. 14 files should explain it at its first use in Marx's voice.
- **ch14-p012** – "working day" (§7, first in chapter) – no gloss – "gaps in his working day" refers to an independent artificer (a craftsman), not a wage worker, so the §7 wording (necessary labor plus surplus labor) would not fit and would add a claim. I treated this as an everyday use. Later Ch. 14 files should explain it at its first technical use.
- **ch14-p012** – "intensity of labor" (§7, first in chapter) – no gloss – Marx defines it in the same sentence ("increased expenditure of labour-power in a given time, i.e., … increased intensity of labour").
- **ch14-p012** – "is made up for by prolonging the duration of the normal velocity when once acquired" – rendered "made up for by keeping up the normal speed for longer once it has been reached". I split it from the "demanded by every transition" clause with "and", which keeps the relation (the extra cost is offset) without adding a cause. "animal spirits" was kept as an image, with no gloss.
- **ch14-p009** – long "either … or, whenever …" sentence – kept as one sentence, so the castes/guilds alternative stays governed by "the tendency shown by earlier societies" and isn't recast as a separate claim about what societies did.
- **ch14-p013** – "productivity of labor" and "labor process" – §7 glosses added at their first use ("productiveness of labour" → "productivity of labor").
- **n. 2, n. 4** – "artists" and "artificers" in quotations – glossed "[craftsmen]" once each, because the older sense of "artists" would mislead a modern reader.
- **ch14-p010** – "warp" and "vicissitude" glossed in brackets inside the quotation. "expedient", "piece goods" and "web" were left unglossed because they are clear from context.
- **n. 4** – typo – "since, they inherit" → "since they inherit": a stray comma with only one reading (§1.4), removed. "Bibl. Hist. I. 1. c., 74." – the "1. c." may be a misprint for "l. c.", but it could also be a book or chapter number, so I kept it exactly as printed and did not expand it.
- **n. 5** – "&c." – it stays inside the book title, and becomes "etc." after the authors' names (Marx's citation prose). "i.e." becomes "that is".
- **Heading** – "Labourer" → "Laborer", with American spelling only (§1.4). I didn't apply the glossary's "worker", because heading wording stays as printed.
