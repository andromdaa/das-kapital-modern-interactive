# Notes for ch23a.md

1. pt7-p001 to pt7-p005 – The file opens with the Part 7 introduction, which sits before Chapter 23 and belongs to no chapter. – Treated it as the start of the unit: the §7 explanations for value, capital, means of production, labor-power, circulation, commodity, surplus-value (pt7-p001) and accumulation of capital (pt7-p002) go at their first use there, and are not repeated in Chapter 23. – The context file lists these as first appearances in this file; repeating them a few paragraphs later would be padding.

2. pt7-p001 – Five §7 explanations fall in the first sentence. – Value, capital, means of production and labor-power were put in one parenthetical block right after the sentence; circulation, commodity and surplus-value were worked in at their own words. – Keeps the first sentence readable without dropping any explanation.

3. ch23-p004 – "simple reproduction" is listed as a first appearance, but Marx defines it himself here (revenue spent entirely on consumption, "a mere repetition of the process of production on the old scale"). – No §7 explanation added. – §7.0: do not add an explanation where Marx gives the definition himself.

4. ch23-p005 – "money form" is used ("the money form of the commodity"). – Added the §7 explanation as a short sentence after the paragraph's last sentence. – First technical use in the chapter.

5. footnote 7 (ch23-p010) – "manufacture" occurs ("the first introducing a manufacture") but in the everyday sense of a manufacturing trade, inside a 1677 quotation. "price" occurs only in a title ("Prices of Labour"). – No §7 explanation for either. – §7.0: everyday use and titles do not count.

6. ch23-p005 – Two §7 terms (wages, variable capital) fall in one sentence. – Wages explained in place; variable capital explained in a separate parenthetical sentence directly after, before "And he stays employed only as long as...". – Avoids two parentheses in one sentence; the order of Marx's claims is unchanged.

7. ch23-p010 – "sine quâ non" in Marx's own voice; not in the §3.4 list. – Rendered "the indispensable condition". – Ordinary-vocabulary Latin tag in Marx's voice (§2.3), not a famous motto.

8. footnote 1 (ch23-p003) – The source gives a bracketed English translation of Sismondi that is loose ("who consume the labour of others" for "les produits du travail des autres"; "with full justification", "capricious wishes" added) and has a typo ("arises our of"). – Kept the French exactly, including Marx's English interpolation "[purchases of commodities]", and replaced the bracket with a literal plain translation (§2.3). The interpolation was not repeated in the translation, as in the source. – Translate what the French says, not a paraphrase; the typo disappears with the retranslation.

9. footnote 5 (ch23-p007) – Marx's interjection "(i.e., the labourer)" inside the Smith quotation. – Rendered "(that is, the worker)"; added the gloss "subject [the material worked on]" after Smith's word. – Interjection is Marx's voice (§0.9); "subject" in this sense misleads modern readers (glossary note on "subject of labour", kept inside quotations with a gloss).

10. pt7-p005 – "obscured by the incident of the circulation which brings it about". – Rendered "obscured by the incidental process of circulation that brings it about". – Keeps both ideas (circulation brings accumulation about; it is incidental to its fundamental form) without choosing between them.

11. pt7-p003 – "He has to share it with capitalists, with landowners, &c." – Kept "with capitalists" without adding "other". – Adding "other" would be an interpretive addition, however obvious.

12. ch23-p003 – "the economic guise of a capitalist attaches to a man". – Rendered "a man wears the economic guise of a capitalist"; kept "guise" (character mask) rather than flattening to "becomes a capitalist". – Simplifying to "becomes" would lose the point that the capitalist role is a social form attached to the person.

13. ch23-p007 – "some other wonderful changes" – Kept "wonderful". – Possible irony; left as printed so neither the literal nor the ironic reading is forced.

14. ch23-p010 – "devoid of all means of making that wealth his own". – Rendered "without any means", not "stripped of". – "stripped" would add an agent and an act that the sentence does not state.
